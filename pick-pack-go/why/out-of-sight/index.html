    Things that go into the suitcase stop existing

    This is the strangest packing problem ADHD creates — the hardest one to explain to anyone who doesn't live with it, and the one that convinced me a generic checklist could never be enough. In the words that come up constantly in ADHD communities: "We often forget what items we've packed once they disappear into the suitcase. It's like they cease to exist."

    So you unpack to check. Then repack. Then wake at 3 AM certain the charger isn't in there, and take everything out again — and in one story I kept thinking about, it was the repacking that left the laptop charger on the bed. I've done the 3 AM excavation myself. The anxiety isn't irrational. For a brain that runs on "out of sight, out of mind," a zipped suitcase is a black box, and the only way to trust a black box is to open it.

    What's actually happening

    "Object permanence" is the community's shorthand for it — not the infant-development milestone, but the everyday adult experience that things outside your visual field lose their grip on your attention entirely. It's why ADHD homes have doom piles and open shelving, and why so many ADHDers pack by spreading everything across the bed first: visible things exist; put-away things are a rumor.

    Packing is uniquely cruel here because its goal state is everything hidden. The better you're doing, the less you can see — and the less your brain believes you've done anything at all.

    Where most apps get this backwards

    A checked checkbox is supposed to be the answer: proof you packed it. But a checkmark you made forty minutes ago has the same problem as the suitcase — it's a past event you have to trust, and ADHD trust in past-you runs thin (reasonably: past-you has ticked a box by accident before). Most packing apps offer exactly one layer of verification, and once every box is ticked, the app's posture is done, stop looking. That does nothing for the 3 AM question, and nothing at all for the trip's second act — the hotel room, where there's no list left to check because you already checked it.

    What Pick, Pack, Go does instead

    Pick, Pack, Go assumes you'll want to be sure twice, and builds the second look in as a feature instead of leaving you to re-dig through the bag.

    • Critical items. Some things ruin a trip if they're missing — medication, passport, keys, the charger. Pick, Pack, Go ships with 33 items already flagged critical, and you can mark anything else. Critical items are visually badged everywhere they appear, so the stuff that matters can't blend into the sock count.
    • Double Check. On the Travel phase — the day you leave — the app automatically gathers every critical item from across your lists into one section at the very bottom, and asks for a separate confirmation tick on each. It's deliberately independent of the original "packed" checkbox: one tick says I packed this at some point, the other says I verified it today, before walking out the door. That distinction is the whole feature. It's the app-shaped version of the pat-down at the front door — phone, wallet, keys, meds — except it knows your personal list.
    • The count that's left, not the pile that's done. Sections and phases show how many items remain, so "am I done?" has an answer you can read instead of a suitcase you have to reopen.
    • The homeward leg. Here's the part almost nothing addresses: the riskiest place to lose something isn't your house — it's a hotel room, where nothing is where it lives and checkout is a deadline. A trip in Pick, Pack, Go isn't over when you arrive. As your return date approaches, the app offers to reset your Pack and Travel lists for the journey home — every physical item unchecked and waiting to be re-verified, while one-way tasks like "run the dishwasher" stay done. Items that don't come home (the house-sitting errands, the "lights off?" checks) know to sit the return out. Staying somewhere with multiple stops? Reset it at every hotel.

    What Pick, Pack, Go doesn't do

    A checkbox is still a checkbox — Pick, Pack, Go can't see inside your bag, and a second confirmation is a second look, not a guarantee. What it removes is the unstructured re-checking: instead of unpacking everything at 3 AM to answer one question, you get a short, purpose-built list of exactly the things worth being sure about.
