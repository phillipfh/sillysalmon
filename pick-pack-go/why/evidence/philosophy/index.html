    Design decisions, defended

    Every significant choice in Pick, Pack, Go, the ADHD challenge it answers, and what backs it.

    Closes: U41 (Xcode Files/docs/deferred/UNFORGET.md), open since 2026-08-21. Written: 2026-09-18 · Public form: this document, rendered, is the Philosophy page at /pick-pack-go/why/evidence/philosophy/.

    How to read the Status column

    This document is honest about what it can and cannot prove today. Four states:

    Meaning
    🟢 CitedA published source is attached. Safe to describe publicly as research-backed.
    🟡 To sourceA source was being sought. No row is in this state any more — WP-1 closed the last of them on 2026-09-18. Kept in the key so the history of this page reads straight.
    ✏️ CorrectedThe research said something different from what this table assumed, so the row was rewritten before it was cited.
    🔵 LivedGrounded in first-person or community experience. A claim about experience, never dressed as science. Legitimate and permanent — some of these should never become 🟢.
    ⚪ ConventionObservably true in the build, but no recorded ruling. The defence would be reverse-engineered from behaviour. Either rule it or label it.

    WP-1 ran on 2026-09-18 and resolved every 🟡 row. Five are now 🟢 — two of them only after the claim behind them was corrected — and five are 🔵, because no adequate source exists and saying so is the honest outcome. Two of the positioning essays changed as a result. Note F records what moved and why; the five per-construct pages under /why/evidence/ carry the sources and, more importantly, the limits.

    A note on the ✏️ marks: they are not failures. A pass that returned no corrections would have meant nobody actually read the papers.

    1. Architecture

    DecisionWhat it looks like in the appChallenge addressedStatus
    Refinement over creationA new trip arrives already populated from a 45-section, 839-item library. Your first act is deleting, not writingStarting and planning are where the measured costs sit — Initiate and Plan/Organize are both clinically elevated on adult self-report, and planning is among the strongest lab findings. That deleting is cheaper than generating is a reasoned design position, not a measured one✏️ → 🟢 Corrected, then cited — see Note F.1
    Five phases per tripPrep · Pack · Travel · Plans · Return, auto-created, four visible at a timePlanning breakdown. Planning is one of the strongest and most consistent executive-function findings in ADHD, and the one adult prospective-memory study to decompose the process located the impairment in task planning specifically🟢 Cited
    TemplatesSave any trip; the next similar trip starts from your defaults, not the app'sRepeated executive-function cost. Executive and working-memory differences persist into adulthood, so a setup cost paid per trip is paid indefinitely🟢 Cited
    Master / instance freezeLibrary edits never touch existing trips; trips never write back to the libraryFear of experimenting. If nothing you do can break anything, exploration stops being risky🔵
    The homeward leg is modelledA Return phase, per-item "needed on the way home," a repeatable Pack/Travel reset, and a one-time offer near the return dateThe trip home is a second packing event that no other app treats as one. Attention has usually left the building by then🔵

    Notes — the rows that need care

    Note A — the 11 silent core sections cut both ways

    They're defensible as structure-you-didn't-have-to-ask-for. But HOW-TO 09 and the docs findings both record the cost: a user can't find them in the picker, and Wallet &amp; Docs — the first core section holding physical objects on the Pack phase — inherits invisibility from a category where invisibility made sense for a different reason (task checklists nobody needs to review).

    Do not defend this row as unambiguously good. The honest framing on a public page is: this is a trade-off we made deliberately, and here's what it costs. That framing is more credible than the alternative, and it's already an open item (U51).

    Note B — sentence case: ruled 2026-09-18, and the library proves it

    The ruling, in the owner's words: "Title Case looks shouty."

    Names are sentence case. Capitalize the first word and proper nouns only. Brand names, trademarks and initialisms keep their own capitalization — Apple Watch, AirTag, Get cash from ATM, Neck gaiter or Buff. Offering alternatives changes nothing: Brush or comb, Hiking shoes or boots.

    This is safe to publish as a decision rather than a convention, because the data proves the practice preceded the ruling. Measured across 863 unique item names on 2026-09-18:

    • 615 of 636 multi-word names were already sentence case
    • Every exception but one was a correct proper noun — Apple Watch, Infant Tylenol, Pack &amp; Play
    • One genuine error, Menstrual Cup, fixed the same day
    • The library is now 100% consistent: 863 names, zero violations

    One thing the ruling corrected. The owner's initial account was that capitals marked alternatives — two similar items offered as a choice. The library disagreed: of 20 names offering alternatives, 19 keep the second option lowercase. Neck gaiter or Buff is capitalized because Buff is a trademark, not because it's an alternative. The proper-noun rule alone covers every case, which is why it's the one that got ruled.

    ⚠️ Still to record in the engineering repo: this ruling belongs in Xcode Files/docs/RULINGS.md. Not added from here — that tree had uncommitted work from another active session on 2026-09-18, and this project has a history of concurrent edits being swept or dropped.

    Driven on a clean install, fresh trip, on a throwaway simulator. What's actually true:

    A critical item's checkbox is replaced by a red exclamationmark.circle. It is not a badge beside the name and not an adornment — it stands exactly where the grey hollow circle stands on an ordinary row, and it is the tap target.

    Confirmed in three places on one screen:

    That last row confirms the On You ruling shipped: the ! treatment does not leak into On You, so two similar-looking confirm controls never appear on one screen.

    The earlier code-reading was a red herring. Template List Collection.swift:389's .systemOrange/.systemRed is the swipe action's background, where colour encodes the action being offered, not the item's state. It has nothing to do with how a critical row renders.

    Publishable sentence: critical items replace their checkbox with a red exclamation mark, so the thing you tap is itself the warning.

    03-object-permanence.md's "visually badged everywhere they appear" is accurate and can stay, though "the checkbox itself becomes a warning" is stronger and now verified.

    Note D — no notifications is a trade-off, and the page should say so

    05-time-blindness.md already states this plainly: no alarms, timers, or notifications. An app about time blindness that doesn't nudge you is a legitimate thing to be challenged on.

    The defence is real — an app that interrupts is an app you mute, and a muted app helps nobody — but it is a design position, not a finding. Label it as one. Do not let WP-1 dress it in a citation.

    Note E — object permanence is community language, not a clinical construct

    The single most important trap in this table.

    Object permanence in the research literature is Piaget's infant-development concept. It is not the mechanism behind "things cease to exist once they're in the suitcase," and citing it that way would be wrong in a way a skeptical reader will catch immediately.

    The defensible constructs are prospective memory and working-memory maintenance.

    03-object-permanence.md may keep the community phrasing — it's genuinely how people describe the experience, and the essay's job is recognition. But the evidence page must cite the real mechanism and say plainly that the popular term and the research term differ. Handled openly, that's a credibility gain: it shows the author knows the difference. Handled quietly, it's the weakest link on the site.

    Note F — what WP-1 changed, 2026-09-18

    The research pass ran against PubMed, Crossref and the publishers directly. Ten 🟡 rows resolved: five to 🟢, five to 🔵. Two essays changed. Every source below is linked from the five evidence pages, and every one of those pages carries a limits section, because the limits are the part that makes the rest worth believing.

    F.1 — "Refinement over creation" was overstated, and is now stated correctly. The old wording called generating structure from nothing "the executive-function bottleneck" and said recognising "not this" is "nearly free." Neither survives contact with the literature. Task initiation is not a domain in the lab-task meta-analyses at all — Willcutt et al.'s strongest domains are response inhibition, vigilance, working memory and planning. Initiation appears on self-report: in treatment-naive adults with newly diagnosed ADHD, four of the five BRIEF-A Metacognition scales were clinically elevated, Initiate among them. That is a real finding and a weaker one than "bottleneck," and it is now described as what it is — an account people give of themselves, not a measured performance deficit. No study has tested whether editing a pre-filled list is easier than writing one, for anyone. The premise the whole app rests on is a design position; it is now labelled as one.

    F.2 — Five rows had no literature behind them, and now say so. Critical items, Quantity formulas, Essentials pre-selected, Double Check and Groups collapsed by default were all marked 🟡 on the assumption a source would turn up. None did, and in most cases none could: "not all forgetting costs the same" is a claim about consequences, and "visual overwhelm" is a description of an experience rather than a construct with a literature. These are good design decisions with honest provenance. Demoting them costs nothing except the ability to call them science, which they never were.

    F.3 — Choice overload is contested, and the table said it like settled fact. A meta-analysis of 63 conditions from 50 experiments (N = 5,036) found "a mean effect size of virtually zero." A later meta-analysis of 99 observations (N = 7,202) found a significant effect once four moderators were accounted for, describing its own result as "counter to the data reported by prior meta-analytic research." Both are good papers; they disagree. Relevance filtering keeps its citation, now stated conditionally and marked contested.

    F.4 — The essays' shakiest paragraph has been rewritten. 04-decision-paralysis.md asserted that "each small decision draws from the same depleted account as the big ones, and the account empties fast." That is the resource model of self-control, whose two large preregistered multi-laboratory replications found d = 0.04 (95% CI [−0.07, 0.15]) and d = 0.10. And there is no ADHD-specific version of it in the literature at all — nothing tests whether decisions cost people with ADHD more. The paragraph now stays in experience and says plainly that the tidy explanation is one of psychology's shakier findings.

    F.5 — Optimistic time estimates are an ordinary human trait. 05-time-blindness.md implied that chronically optimistic time estimates are part of the ADHD picture. Underestimating your own task durations is the planning fallacy, demonstrated in the general population, and I could find no research on task-duration prediction in ADHD specifically. The essay now says so. Separately: the ADHD timing literature measures milliseconds to seconds, while the essay is about days — the two are not the same claim, and the evidence page says which is which.

    F.6 — Prospective memory supports the out-of-sight essay less tidily than expected. Note E's ruling stands: object permanence is Piaget's infant construct and prospective memory is the right replacement. But the one adult study to decompose prospective memory found the impairment concentrated in task planning, with "only negligible to small effects" for plan recall, self-initiation and execution — and the child literature (six studies) mostly found time-based rather than event-based deficits, where packing cues are largely event-based. The evidence page states this rather than burying it. Working-memory maintenance is doing most of the real work on that page.

    F.7 — Still outstanding, and not WP-1's to close. The sentence-case ruling in Note B is still not recorded in Xcode Files/docs/RULINGS.md (verified 2026-09-18: zero matches). Until it is, the public defence is the owner's quoted ruling plus the library measurement, which is enough to publish but is not yet citable to the project's own design system.
