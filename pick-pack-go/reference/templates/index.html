    The shape of the idea

    A template is a trip with the trip-specific parts removed. Everything about how you pack is kept; everything about one particular journey is dropped.

    That's why the omissions matter more than the inclusions. A template that remembered your dates would be a copy of a trip. A template that forgets them is a description of how you travel.

    Ticks are never captured

    A template always arrives fresh. There is no way to save a half-packed state, which is deliberate: a template carrying someone else's progress would be worse than useless on the night you need it.

    Nominal length

    Every template holds a length in nights. It does two things at two different moments, and only one of them is obvious.

    At authoring time it becomes a trip-duration category that filters which library items seed into the template at all.

    At trip creation it pre-fills the return date and seeds the auto-adjust window.

    So a "Ski Week" template built without noticing the duration control gets a 3-night item set — the silent default — and later produces a 3-night trip. Neither moment says so. (Tracked as U62. The ask is legibility at the point of effect, not removing the default; fewer choices is the design for this audience.)

    Quantities travel as rules, not numbers

    A template stores the formula, so quantities compute against the dates of whatever trip you make from it. One template correctly clothes a weekend and a fortnight.

    See Quantity formulas → Templates and length.

    Customisation survives

    Everything you changed is kept: renamed sections, added items, adjusted quantities, notes. That's the whole point — the executive-function cost of setting a trip up gets paid once rather than every time.

    Where templates diverge from the library

    A template is a snapshot, exactly like a trip. Editing your library does not reach into templates that already exist, and editing a template does not write back.

    This is the most common source of the "my three libraries have drifted apart" feeling: the Template, Section and Item libraries are independent snapshots by design, and nothing reconciles them automatically.
