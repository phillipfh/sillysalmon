    What Pick, Pack, Go is, in about thirty seconds.

    The gist

    • Your list starts full, not empty. Make a trip and you get a packing list already built from a library of 863 items across 36 ready-made lists, with kits you add as you need them.
    • Your job is deleting, not writing. Crossing things out is easy. Staring at a blank list is not. The whole app is built around that difference.
    • A trip has five phases — Prep, Pack, Travel, Plans, Return — so "packing" stops being one enormous task and becomes five small ones that arrive when they're relevant.
    • Nothing you do in a trip changes your library, and nothing in your library changes a trip you've already made. You can experiment freely.
    • It all lives on your phone. No account, no sign-in, nothing to set up.

    The one idea

    Most packing apps hand you an empty list and a search box. That's the wrong way round for a brain that struggles to start: the blank page is the hard part, and it's where the whole thing stalls.

    Pick, Pack, Go goes the other way. You tell it roughly what kind of trip this is, and it hands you a list that's already too long. Then you scroll and delete. Recognising "I don't need hiking boots" takes a second. Remembering hiking boots exist, unprompted, at 11pm, takes a functioning executive system you may not have available that night.

    If you only remember one thing: when the list feels overwhelming, you're meant to be cutting it down, not filling it in.

    Your first five minutes

    Go straight to Pack for a weekend trip in five minutes. It walks the whole thing end to end, from an empty Home screen to a list you can work down.

    What this app doesn't do

    Worth knowing up front, so you're not waiting for something that isn't coming.

    • It never notifies you. No reminders, no alerts, no countdown. Nothing will buzz to tell you it's time to pack. If you need a nudge, set an alarm the way you normally would.
    • It doesn't sync between devices. Your data lives on this iPhone. It's included in your iCloud device backup, so a new phone restored from backup gets everything — but two phones won't stay in step with each other.
    • It doesn't share. No collaborators, no sending a list to someone else.
    • It doesn't know the weather. You tell it whether the trip is warm or cold; it doesn't look anything up.
    • There's no web or iPad version. iPhone only, for now.

    Everything else on these pages is real and shipping. If a page describes something you can't find, that's a bug in the docs — not something you're missing.
